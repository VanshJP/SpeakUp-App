Sixty seconds of talking tells you what is holding your speaking back, and what to fix first. No account, nothing uploaded, all on your iPhone. Free for 14 days.

=== DESCRIPTION (4000 max) ===

You already know you say "um" too much. You do not know how often, where it happens, or what to do about it. Big Talk answers all three in about a minute.

Talk to your phone for sixty seconds. Get back the recording you were too nervous to listen to, marked up: every filler word in place, your pace charted second by second, and one thing to work on next.

HEAR WHAT EVERYONE ELSE HEARS

The gap between how you think you sound and how you actually sound is where most of the damage lives. Big Talk closes it with numbers instead of opinions — clarity, pace, pauses, delivery, vocabulary, and structure, each with a plain-language reason behind the score. You find out which one is dragging the rest down, and you stop guessing.

STOP SAYING UM

A live counter ticks up while you are still talking, so you hear the habit in the moment instead of in the edit. Afterwards, every filler sits highlighted in your transcript where you said it. People break the habit when they can see it — that is the whole trick.

SLOW DOWN WITHOUT SOUNDING SLOW

Nervous speakers speed up and never notice. Your pace gets charted second by second, so you can see the exact sentence where you took off. Then the pace drill puts you inside a target range and holds you there until steady feels normal.

LEARN THE DIFFERENCE BETWEEN A PAUSE AND A STALL

Good speakers pause more than bad ones. Big Talk separates the deliberate beat from the moment you lost your place, and scores them differently, because one of them is a skill and the other is a tell.

WALK IN KNOWING YOUR SPEECH COLD

Paste in what you actually have to say — the wedding toast, the interview answer, the stakeholder update, the pitch — and Big Talk scores how closely you delivered what you wrote, not how well you matched a generic prompt. Practice it enough times and the day stops being the first time you have said it out loud.

Not sure what to practice? Hundreds of prompts, from interview questions to impromptu topics, so you never sit there deciding.

WATCH YOURSELF GET BETTER

Improvement in speaking is real but slow enough to feel like nothing is happening. Put your first session next to your latest and it stops being invisible: charts for every metric, streaks, milestones, a practice heatmap, and Home Screen widgets that keep the habit in front of you.

An eight-week curriculum takes you from your first recorded minute to structured, unhurried delivery, and advances on what your sessions actually show rather than on a button you tapped.

PRACTICE BADLY WITH NOBODY WATCHING

The reason people don't rehearse out loud is that someone might hear it. Nobody will. Transcription and scoring happen on your iPhone using on-device speech models, and your recordings, transcripts, and scores never leave it. There is no account, no ad network, and no analytics service — just a coarse usage log on the device, which you can read and delete in Settings. Progress cards you choose to share carry scores and dates only, never a transcript, a prompt, or your audio.

After that you keep three full analyses every 30 days, plus every drill, warm-up, read-aloud passage, your own scripts, and the progress cards.

Nothing charges by itself: there is no subscription and no card involved.
